### Air fryers
Q: Which air fryer should I buy?
A: For most people, the Ninja Foodi Dual Zone AF400UK. The default pick for a family. The Sync function is the thing people actually keep using: chips in one drawer, chicken in the other, both done at once. Buy it if You cook for 3 to 5 people, or you want a main and a side ready together without juggling one basket.

Q: What size air fryer do I need for a family of 4?
A: Aim for 7 to 9L total. A dual-basket like the Ninja Foodi Dual Zone (9.5L across two drawers) is the common family choice because it cooks a main and a side at once. A single 6L basket also works if you don't mind cooking in one space.

Q: Is a dual-basket air fryer worth it?
A: Yes, if your problem is timing: getting two foods ready at the same moment. Dual baskets let you run two drawers at different temperatures and finish together. If you mostly cook one thing at a time, a single basket is simpler and cheaper.

Q: Does wattage matter when buying an air fryer?
A: Barely. Within the same size class, air fryers are close enough on power that it won't change your cooking. Size and basket layout matter far more than watts.

Q: How many litres of air fryer do I need per person?
A: Roughly 2L per person as a floor, but think in household bands: 3.5 to 4L for one, 4 to 5L for two, 7 to 9L for a family of four. What you cook matters as much as how many you feed.

Q: Is a 9L air fryer too big for 2 people?
A: Usually yes. A 9L dual-basket takes serious worktop space and you'd rarely use the second drawer. For two people a 4 to 5L single basket is a better fit.

Q: What is the best air fryer for a family of 4?
A: A dual-basket around 7 to 9L. The Ninja Foodi Dual Zone AF400UK (9.5L, two drawers) is the common family default because it cooks a main and a side to finish together. The Tefal Easy Fry Dual is the lower-budget alternative.

Q: Can one air fryer cook a full family meal?
A: A dual-basket can: one drawer for your main, one for the side, finishing at the same time. A single basket can too, but you'll cook in sequence, which is slower for a family.

### Cordless vacuums
Q: Which cordless vacuum should I buy?
A: For most people, the Dyson V8. The value Dyson, and the one most people should actually buy. Lighter and far cheaper than the V15, still more than enough for most homes. Buy it if You want a Dyson that's light and reliable without the flagship price, in a flat or small-to-medium house.

Q: What is the best cordless vacuum in the UK?
A: There's no single best; it depends on your home. For most people the Dyson V8 is the sensible default: light, strong and far cheaper than the flagship. Pet owners should look at the Shark Stratos for its anti-hair-wrap brush, and anyone who wants the deepest clean and has the budget should consider the Dyson V15 Detect.

Q: How much runtime do I really need in a cordless vacuum?
A: For a flat, 30 to 40 minutes is plenty. For a three-bed house with carpet, aim for 50+ minutes or a model with a removable or second battery, because powered carpet cleaning drains the battery far faster than the headline 'eco' figure.

Q: Does suction (air watts) matter most when buying a cordless vacuum?
A: No. Above a sensible level, more suction mainly shortens runtime. Brush-head design, weight and anti-hair-wrap features make a bigger difference to how clean your floors actually get.

Q: Is a cordless vacuum under £150 any good?
A: Yes, for a flat or small house. Expect 30 to 45 minutes of real runtime and solid suction. The main thing to check is a removable battery, so the vacuum isn't scrap when the battery fades. The Vax ONEPWR Blade 4 is a sensible pick in this range.

Q: What should I avoid in a budget cordless vacuum?
A: Avoid models with a sealed, non-replaceable battery, and ignore huge headline suction claims from unknown brands. A swappable battery and a decent brush head matter far more than the number on the box.

Q: What is the best cordless vacuum for pet hair in the UK?
A: For most pet owners the Shark Stratos Anti Hair Wrap is the best value: its anti-hair-wrap brush stops hair tangling, and two batteries do a whole house. If budget allows, the Dyson V15 Detect adds the strongest suction and is better for allergy sufferers.

Q: Do I need special features for pet hair?
A: Yes, one in particular: an anti-hair-wrap brush roll. It's more important than suction, because it stops pet hair wrapping around the brush and clogging it. An easy-empty bin and good filtration help too, especially for allergies.

### Robot vacuums
Q: Which robot vacuum should I buy?
A: For most people, the eufy X10 Pro Omni. The value all-in-one, and the default pick for most homes. Empties itself and washes its own mop for around half the flagship price. Buy it if You want the hands-off life (it empties itself and washes its own mop) without paying flagship money.

Q: What is the best robot vacuum in the UK?
A: There's no single best; it depends on budget. For most people the eufy X10 Pro Omni is the sensible default: it empties itself and washes its own mop for around half the flagship price. On a budget, the iRobot Roomba 115 Combo gives you a self-emptying dock cheaply; for the very best clean, the Roborock Saros 20 leads on suction and mopping.

Q: Is a self-emptying robot vacuum worth it?
A: For most people, yes. A self-emptying base means weeks of hands-off cleaning instead of emptying a small bin every day or two. It's the feature most worth paying for, more so than extra suction. Budget models under about £300 usually skip it.

Q: How much suction (Pa) do I need in a robot vacuum?
A: Less than the marketing suggests. Above a sensible level, more Pa mainly drains the battery. It matters most for thick carpet and pet hair; on hard floors almost any modern robot is fine. Brush design and a self-emptying dock make a bigger day-to-day difference.

Q: Can you get a good robot vacuum under £300?
A: Yes. Under £300 you can get proper laser navigation and solid cleaning, and with the iRobot Roomba 115 Combo you even get a self-emptying dock. You mainly give up the strongest suction and the self-washing mop stations found on £500+ models.

Q: Can you get a self-emptying robot vacuum under £300?
A: Yes, but choose carefully. The iRobot Roomba 115 Combo pairs a self-emptying AutoEmpty dock with a budget price. Most other sub-£300 robots skip the dock, so you empty a small onboard bin yourself.

Q: What is the best robot vacuum for pet hair in the UK?
A: If budget allows, the Roborock Saros 20: its class-leading suction lifts pet hair out of carpet and its base is hands-off for weeks. The best value for pet owners is the eufy X10 Pro Omni, which empties and washes itself, so shedding is handled without daily effort.

Q: What features matter for pet hair in a robot vacuum?
A: Two above all: strong suction (to pull hair out of carpet) and a self-emptying dock (so the small bin doesn't fill up every day with a shedding pet). An anti-tangle brush design also helps stop hair wrapping around the roller.

### Dehumidifiers
Q: Which dehumidifier should I buy?
A: For most people, the Pro Breeze 20L OmniDry. The sensible default for most UK homes. A Which? Best Buy, quiet enough for a bedroom, and strong on damp, condensation and drying clothes. Buy it if You want one dependable unit for a normal heated home that handles damp, condensation and the odd load of washing.

Q: What is the best dehumidifier in the UK?
A: It depends on the room. For most heated homes the Pro Breeze 20L OmniDry is the sensible default: a Which? Best Buy, quiet enough for a bedroom, strong on damp and laundry. For a cold or unheated space a desiccant like the EcoAir DD1 Classic or Meaco DD8L is the better choice, and for a big or very damp house the Pro Breeze 30L pulls the most.

Q: Is a compressor or desiccant dehumidifier better?
A: Neither is better overall; it depends on the room. A compressor pulls more water and uses less electricity in a warm, heated home, so it's the default there. A desiccant is lighter, quieter and keeps working in a cold or unheated space (garage, conservatory, caravan) where a compressor struggles. Match the type to whether your problem room is warm or cold.

Q: What size dehumidifier do I need?
A: Roughly: one room or a small flat needs about 12 litres a day, a typical 2 to 3 bed home about 20, and a big or very damp house 25 to 30. The litres-per-day figure is measured in warm, damp lab conditions, so real-world extraction is lower; size up a little if your home is cold or badly affected.

Q: What is the best dehumidifier for drying clothes in the UK?
A: For a normal heated room, the Pro Breeze 20L OmniDry: a Which? Best Buy with a laundry mode, quiet enough to leave on. If you dry washing in a cold or unheated room, the Meaco DD8L desiccant dries it fast where a compressor would struggle. The De'Longhi AriaDry Multi is a strong premium alternative.

Q: Does a dehumidifier dry clothes faster?
A: Yes. In a closed room with an airer, a dehumidifier pulls the moisture straight out of the air, so clothes dry noticeably faster than on an airer alone, and far more cheaply than tumble drying. It also stops that moisture settling on cold walls and windows as condensation and mould.

Q: What is the best dehumidifier for damp and condensation?
A: For most heated homes, the Pro Breeze 20L OmniDry, a quiet Which? Best Buy. For a single room or small flat the Pro Breeze 12L is enough and cheaper; for a big or very damp house the Pro Breeze 30L pulls the most; and for a cold garage or conservatory a desiccant like the EcoAir DD1 Classic keeps working where a compressor stalls.

Q: Will a dehumidifier stop condensation and mould?
A: Yes, used right. Condensation forms when moist air meets cold surfaces, and mould needs that damp to grow. A dehumidifier holding the room at around 50 to 55 percent humidity removes the moisture, so condensation stops forming and mould loses the conditions it needs. Existing mould still needs cleaning off.
