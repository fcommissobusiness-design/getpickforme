Q: Does cooking for one in an air fryer actually save money?

Q: What is the best air fryer for frozen chips UK?
A: For a family, the Ninja Foodi Dual Zone AF400UK. Both drawers hit 240°C so frozen chips genuinely crisp, and you can do chips and nuggets together with Sync Finish. For one or two people, the Cosori 4.7L Compact gets to 230°C fast and a 500g bag of frozen chips fits without doubling up.

Q: What temperature do you cook frozen chips in an air fryer?
A: 200°C for 15 to 18 minutes, shaking halfway. Thinner shoestring chips need closer to 12 to 14 minutes; chunkier McCain Home Chips take the full 18. No preheat needed - you save 3 minutes and the chips are no worse.

Q: Do you need to preheat an air fryer for frozen food?
A: For most frozen food, no. The fryer reaches cooking temperature in roughly 3 to 4 minutes anyway, and the food is thawing in the first minute. The exception is steak or a sear, where you want the basket already screaming hot - but that's not frozen cooking.

Q: Can you cook frozen nuggets and chips together in an air fryer?
A: Yes, but they need different times. Chips take 15 to 18 minutes at 200°C; nuggets take 10 to 12. In a single basket, start the chips, then add the nuggets after 6 or 7 minutes so they finish together. In a dual-basket like the Ninja AF400UK, set each drawer separately and use Sync Finish.

Q: Do you put oil on frozen food in the air fryer?
A: Not on pre-breaded or coated food (nuggets, fish fingers, samosas, chips) - they already have oil in the coating. Extra oil runs off and smokes. The exception is plain frozen vegetables (broccoli, sprouts), where a teaspoon of oil helps them brown.

Q: Why are my air-fried frozen chips soggy?
A: Three usual reasons: the basket was overcrowded (two layers of chips steam each other), you didn't shake halfway (chips brown unevenly), or the temperature was set too low (180°C is 'crisp enough', 200°C is 'actually crispy'). The fix is almost always 200°C, half a basket, one shake.

Q: Do robot vacuums actually mop well?
A: They mop lightly, not deeply. Spinning or rolling mop systems (Roborock, eufy C28) apply more pressure than a flat vibrating pad, so they come closest to a real clean.

Q: What's the difference between a robot that mops and one that washes its own mop?

Q: Can a robot vacuum mop and carpet in the same run?

Robots without mop lifting will drag a wet pad across carpet, so on those you set no-go zones for rugs or mop and vacuum in separate runs.

Q: Is a self-washing mop dock worth the extra money?

Q: Do I need hot water mopping in a robot vacuum?

Q: Is a compressor or desiccant dehumidifier better?
A: Neither is better overall; it depends on the room. A compressor pulls more water and uses less electricity in a warm, heated home, so it's the default there. A desiccant is lighter, quieter and keeps working in a cold or unheated space (garage, conservatory, caravan) where a compressor struggles. Match the type to whether your problem room is warm or cold.

Q: What size dehumidifier do I need?
A: Roughly: one room or a small flat needs about 12 litres a day, a typical 2 to 3 bed home about 20, and a big or very damp house 25 to 30. The litres-per-day figure is measured in warm, damp lab conditions, so real-world extraction is lower; size up a little if your home is cold or badly affected.

Q: Does a dehumidifier dry clothes faster?
A: Yes. In a closed room with an airer, a dehumidifier pulls the moisture straight out of the air, so clothes dry noticeably faster than on an airer alone, and far more cheaply than tumble drying. It also stops that moisture settling on cold walls and windows as condensation and mould.

Q: Will a dehumidifier stop condensation and mould?
A: Yes, used right. Condensation forms when moist air meets cold surfaces, and mould needs that damp to grow. A dehumidifier holding the room at around 50 to 55 percent humidity removes the moisture, so condensation stops forming and mould loses the conditions it needs. Existing mould still needs cleaning off.

Q: How quiet does a bedroom dehumidifier need to be?
A: Under 45dB on its lowest setting is the threshold most people can sleep through. The high 30s to low 40s is ideal. Look for a quoted noise figure (not just 'quiet' in the marketing) and a night or sleep mode that dims the display, because a bright LED in the dark is as disruptive as the hum. The Dreo 20L at 38dB is the current benchmark.

Q: Is a dehumidifier safe to leave on all night in the bedroom?
A: Yes. Modern dehumidifiers have overheat protection and shut off automatically when the tank is full, and running one at a target of 50 percent humidity actually cycles it on and off rather than running flat out. The only thing to get right is position: a metre or two from the bed, not pointing at you, and with air clearance around the top vent.
