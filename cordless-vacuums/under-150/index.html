Cordless vacuums guide

Best cordless vacuum under £150

Under £150 you can get a genuinely good cordless, as long as you match it to a flat or small house and don't expect flagship runtime.

What to expect at this price

Under £150 you're buying for a flat or a small house, not a four-bed with carpet throughout. You'll get solid suction and 30 to 45 minutes of real-world runtime. What you give up versus a flagship is deep-carpet power and the fancy brush tech, not basic cleaning ability.

The picks under £150

  • Best value overall: Vax ONEPWR Blade 4. A UK brand, sensible power, and a removable battery you can swap or replace, which stretches the machine's life.
  • If you can stretch slightly: an older Dyson V8 sometimes dips near this range in sales. It's lighter and a step up on carpet, so it's worth watching the price.

What to avoid cheaping out on

Very cheap no-name sticks often have a sealed battery that can't be replaced, so when it fades the whole vacuum is done. A removable battery is the single feature most worth paying a little more for at this price.

Common questions

Is a cordless vacuum under £150 any good?

Yes, for a flat or small house. Expect 30 to 45 minutes of real runtime and solid suction. The main thing to check is a removable battery, so the vacuum isn't scrap when the battery fades. The Vax ONEPWR Blade 4 is a sensible pick in this range.

What should I avoid in a budget cordless vacuum?

Avoid models with a sealed, non-replaceable battery, and ignore huge headline suction claims from unknown brands. A swappable battery and a decent brush head matter far more than the number on the box.
