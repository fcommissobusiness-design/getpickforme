Air fryer size guide: what litres do you actually need?

The single most common air fryer mistake is the wrong size. Here's how capacity maps to real households, so you buy once.

Litres per household, not per person

Capacity doesn't scale neatly per head, because it depends on what you cook, not just how many you feed. Use household bands:

HouseholdSensible capacityBasket type
1 person3.5 to 4LSingle
2 people4 to 5LSingle
3 people5 to 7LSingle or small dual
4 people7 to 9LDual basket
5+ / batch9L+Dual, or cook in rounds

Why "litres" is misleading on dual baskets

A "9.5L" dual-basket is two 4.75L drawers, not one 9.5L space. You can't fit a 9L joint in it. What you get is two medium spaces running at once. That's the right tool for a family dinner, the wrong one for a single large item.

The worktop reality check

Before you buy the biggest one "to be safe", measure your counter. A large dual-basket has a footprint close to a microwave and needs clearance above it for the hot exhaust. The size that fits your kitchen beats the size that fits a hypothetical dinner party.

Once you know your size band, see which air fryer fits in the main guide.

Common questions

How many litres of air fryer do I need per person?

Roughly 2L per person as a floor, but think in household bands: 3.5 to 4L for one, 4 to 5L for two, 7 to 9L for a family of four. What you cook matters as much as how many you feed.

Is a 9L air fryer too big for 2 people?

Usually yes. A 9L dual-basket takes serious worktop space and you'd rarely use the second drawer. For two people a 4 to 5L single basket is a better fit.
